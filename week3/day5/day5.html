Cybersecurity in the Digital Age

As society becomes increasingly dependent on digital infrastructure, the threat posed by cybercrime has grown from a niche technical concern into a (1) mainstream security challenge affecting governments, corporations and ordinary individuals alike. The scale of the problem is striking: according to industry estimates, cybercrime costs the global economy trillions of dollars annually, making it one of the most significant threats to economic stability in the modern era.

At the heart of most cyberattacks lies a fundamental (2) vulnerability — the human element. While organisations invest heavily in technical defences such as firewalls and encryption, research consistently shows that the majority of successful breaches exploit not system weaknesses but human (3) error. Phishing emails, for example, deceive employees into revealing sensitive credentials by impersonating trusted sources, a technique that remains remarkably effective despite widespread awareness campaigns.

The challenge is compounded by the (4) asymmetry of the threat landscape. Attackers need only find one weakness to succeed, whereas defenders must simultaneously protect every possible entry point. This imbalance has driven many organisations to adopt a (5) proactive rather than reactive approach, investing in threat intelligence, regular penetration testing and continuous employee training.

Governments are increasingly recognising that cybersecurity is not merely a corporate responsibility but a matter of national (6) security. Critical infrastructure such as power grids, water treatment facilities and financial systems represent attractive targets for state-sponsored actors seeking to cause maximum disruption. Several high-profile incidents in recent years have demonstrated the potentially (7) catastrophic consequences of successful attacks on such systems, prompting urgent calls for international cooperation and stronger regulatory (8) frameworks.

Answer — NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS from the passage:

1. What has cybercrime grown into, beyond a technical concern?
2. What do most successful breaches exploit rather than system weaknesses?
3. What technique do phishing emails use to deceive employees?
4. What approach have many organisations adopted to deal with the imbalance?
5. What type of infrastructure represents attractive targets for state-sponsored actors?
6. What do recent incidents demonstrate about successful attacks?
7. What are governments calling for to address the cybersecurity threat?

Answers: 1-mainstream security challenge, 2-human error, 3-impersonating trusted sources, 4-proactive (approach), 5-critical infrastructure, 6-catastrophic consequences, 7-international cooperation/regulatory frameworks
