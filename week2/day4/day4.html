The Hidden Dangers of Ultra-Processed Foods

Modern diets have undergone a dramatic transformation over the past few decades. Where once people consumed predominantly (1) whole foods prepared at home, today a significant proportion of caloric intake in many countries comes from ultra-processed products. These are defined as industrial formulations made from substances (2) extracted from foods, such as oils, fats, starches and proteins, combined with additives including preservatives, artificial colours and (3) flavourings.

Research published in leading medical journals has consistently linked high consumption of ultra-processed foods to a range of serious health conditions, including obesity, type 2 diabetes and certain forms of cancer. One particularly alarming finding is that these products are specifically engineered to override the body's natural (4) satiety signals — the mechanisms that tell us we are full — making (5) overconsumption almost inevitable for regular consumers.

Critics of the food industry argue that aggressive marketing, particularly to children, combined with the low cost and convenience of these products, makes reducing consumption exceptionally difficult. Furthermore, many nutritional labels are deliberately misleading, highlighting certain (6) beneficial ingredients while concealing the overall poor quality of the product. Public health experts therefore insist that government (7) intervention is now essential, including clearer labelling requirements, restrictions on advertising and taxation of the most harmful products.

Answer — NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS from the passage:

1. What type of foods did people mainly eat before the modern diet shift?
2. What are preservatives and artificial colours examples of?
3. What natural bodily mechanism do ultra-processed foods interfere with?
4. What makes reducing consumption of ultra-processed foods particularly hard?
5. What do public health experts say governments must now do?
6. What do critics say nutritional labels often hide?
7. What three specific measures do experts recommend?

Answers: 1-whole foods, 2-additives, 3-satiety signals, 4-low cost/convenience, 5-government intervention, 6-overall poor quality, 7-clearer labelling / restrictions on advertising / taxation
