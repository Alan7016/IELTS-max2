The Psychology of Eating

The relationship between the human mind and food is far more complex than many people realise. While nutrition science tends to focus on (1) calories and macronutrients, a growing body of research suggests that psychological factors play an equally significant role in determining what, when, and how much we eat. Emotions such as stress, anxiety and boredom are among the most powerful (2) triggers for unhealthy eating behaviours, a phenomenon researchers refer to as emotional eating.

One particularly well-documented finding is that individuals under chronic stress tend to (3) crave foods that are high in fat and sugar. This is partly because such foods temporarily stimulate the release of dopamine — a neurotransmitter associated with pleasure and (4) reward — creating a short-term sense of comfort. However, this relief is typically followed by feelings of guilt and a subsequent drop in mood, which can in turn (5) perpetuate the cycle of emotional eating.

Environmental factors also exert a powerful influence over our food choices. Research has consistently shown that people consume significantly more food when eating in large groups, when portions are (6) oversized, or when they are distracted by screens. Conversely, practising (7) mindful eating — paying full, deliberate attention to the experience of eating — has been shown to reduce overconsumption and improve satisfaction.

Answer — NO MORE THAN THREE WORDS from the passage:

1. Apart from calories, what does research say equally affects what we eat?
2. What are stress, anxiety and boredom described as in relation to unhealthy eating?
3. What do stressed individuals tend to want more of?
4. What neurotransmitter is linked to pleasure when eating fatty or sugary foods?
5. What does the cycle of emotional eating do over time?
6. What type of eating involves paying full attention to the experience?
7. Name ONE environmental factor that causes people to eat more.

Answers: 1-psychological factors, 2-(powerful) triggers, 3-fat and sugar/crave fatty foods, 4-dopamine, 5-perpetuate (the cycle), 6-mindful eating, 7-large groups / oversized portions / screens
